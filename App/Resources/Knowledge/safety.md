# Safety practices

## Personal protective equipment (PPE)

Eye protection is non-negotiable any time the spindle is running — chips
and dust travel fast and unpredictably, especially from harder materials
or when a tool breaks. Hearing protection is worth using for longer
sessions in metal. A dust mask or respirator matters most for MDF and
other sheet goods with resin binders, and for any material you're not
certain is dust-safe to breathe.

## No loose clothing, jewelry, gloves, or long hair near a running spindle

A spinning tool or collet can catch loose sleeves, gloves, jewelry (rings,
bracelets, lanyards), or untied long hair and pull a hand or head toward
the cutter far faster than a person can react — this is one of the most
serious and most preventable injury risks around any rotating machine tool,
not specific to CNC. Gloves in particular feel like a safety measure but
are actively dangerous near a spindle for exactly this reason; keep hands
bare and away from the tool while it's running.

## Workholding

Secure the workpiece before starting the spindle, not after — a part that
shifts mid-cut can bind the tool, snap it, or become a projectile. Leave
uncut tabs on a cutout until a final separate pass so the part can't come
loose and start moving while it's still being cut around its perimeter.
Check clamps and tape/vacuum hold at the start of every job, not just the
first time a fixture was set up.

## Dust and chip management

Beyond the health angle covered under PPE, accumulated chips/dust around
the spindle and rails can jam moving parts, obscure the cut line from view
(hiding a developing problem), and in wood dust's case represent a fire
risk near hot electronics or a running spindle motor. Running dust
collection or at minimum periodically clearing the work area matters for
both safety and cut quality.

## Awareness of the emergency stop

Know where the machine's e-stop (or, on a bare hobby setup, the power
switch) is and keep it within easy reach before starting any job, not
after something goes wrong. Anything unexpected — an unusual sound, visible
chatter, smoke, a part shifting — is worth stopping for immediately rather
than waiting to see if it resolves itself.

## This app's own limits

Every cutting recommendation this app produces (feed rate, spindle speed,
depth of cut) is a deterministic starting point to verify with a light
test cut, not a guaranteed-safe value for your specific machine, tool
condition, and material batch. Real machines vary in rigidity, tool
sharpness varies between individual bits, and material properties vary
between batches and suppliers.
